FOR A PROJECT REVIEW OR HEARING

What changes for the passenger?

Ask the sponsor to describe the journey after the project opens. Which trains would be added? What transfers would improve? Then ask which other projects, agreements and operating funds are needed for that journey to be possible.

How to read the atlas

What a frequency means

30 minutes means two departures per hour per direction in a uniform service pattern. Headways are planning goals, not a guarantee at every stop or across every destination pair. “≤15” is a frequency band, not an exact 15-minute timetable. Limited, daily and seasonal services are not converted to invented regular intervals. Their fine strokes indicate unquantified/context service—not an inferred headway longer than 240 minutes.

When services share a line

Regional, intercity and HSR layers can use a shared corridor. Their stopping patterns, phasing and departure spacing differ. The atlas does not add them into an implied regular headway. Some table values are explicitly combined service goals; those cards say so.

Geographic references

The map uses California Albers (EPSG:3310). State/shoreline and lake data: Natural Earth. County context: US Atlas / Census-derived cartographic boundaries. Relief: Mapzen Terrain Tiles / AWS Open Data. Rail GIS is a reference dataset—not an as-built inventory or an up-to-date service database.

What the map includes

The atlas maps 70 strategic service-goal layers, four gray existing long-distance Amtrak routes and 97 selected city/hub anchors. Urban metro/light rail is summarized through hub connections; this is not every station or feeder bus. The Amtrak baseline uses Appendix 3.1’s 2024 inventory: Coast Starlight, California Zephyr and Southwest Chief daily; Sunset Limited three days per week. They remain background in every horizon, not forecasts of future service. Optional physical rail context does not imply active passenger service or freight volumes.

Dates, costs and funding

2028, 2034 and 2050 are the plan’s horizons. The map’s lines indicate service goals, not secured funding, an opening forecast or a construction status. Cost figures are 2024 planning figures. Project-specific elected-official actions are this atlas’s implementation synthesis.

What a station dot means

Dots identify selected cities and connection hubs, not a full stopping pattern. Existing station locations are geographic anchors; future hubs are approximate. Parallel corridors are generalized, and close service layers may be cartographically offset for legibility. Exact train paths and future track layouts are not resolved.

AN UNRESOLVED DETAIL IN THE SOURCE

Santa Barbara–Los Angeles: 30 or 60 minutes?

The long-term narrative on p. 34 describes combined half-hourly regional and intercity service. Table 1 on p. 38 describes coordinated hourly service. The atlas uses 60 minutes from the table, marks it with an asterisk, and retains the discrepancy on the corridor card. This difference would need to be resolved when an operating plan is prepared.
